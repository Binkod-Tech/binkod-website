Device Matrix, Explained: What It Shows and How It Helps Your Phone

Device Matrix: System Info brings the details Android exposes about your phone into one place. Here is what it reads, what the guided checks and reports do, and how those readings can help with the device you use.

October 2026 9 min read Product guide

An Android phone holds far more information than the average settings screen makes easy to find. Processor details, memory use, storage, battery state, Wi-Fi information, installed apps and hardware-test results are often scattered across different menus and hidden developer screens.

Device Matrix: System Info is an Android utility that brings those readings together. It also adds guided hardware checks, local reports and home-screen widgets. This guide explains what the app does, where its limits are, and how the information can be useful in ordinary situations.

What Device Matrix is

Device Matrix reads information that Android makes available to normal apps. It presents that information on clear screens instead of filling a page with raw labels. When a value is unavailable on a particular phone or Android version, the app says so rather than inventing a number. Estimated values, such as device age or battery health, are marked as estimates.

The app does not require an account. There is no Device Matrix backend for readings, no cloud storage for your information and no analytics service inside the app. Information is processed on the phone. A report leaves only when you copy, share, save or export it.

Device Matrix is built for Android 8.0 and later. Some brands and Android versions expose more information than others, so two phones can show different levels of detail. That is normal. The app is designed around the readings the device can actually supply.

Hardware

Device and components

Model and build details, processor, graphics, memory, storage, battery, thermal state, display, cameras and sensors.

Network

Connections and data use

Active Wi-Fi and mobile information, operator details, data-usage summaries and network identifiers that stay masked by default.

Apps

Installed-app context

Target Android API, installer sources, declared permissions and screen-time information where Android reports it.

Checks

Tests, reports and widgets

Guided checks for hardware, a report you can preview before sharing, and RAM, storage, battery and health widgets.

Readings stay on the phone

Device Matrix has no account and no server for device readings. Ads and optional Google Play purchases use Google services, but your hardware, network and test information is not uploaded to Binkod Tech.

The main screens, at a glance

The core screens follow the same idea: show a clear reading, give it context, and avoid pretending that missing information exists. These six captures cover the first questions about a phone: Home, Hardware, Thermal, Battery, Storage and Memory. Use the arrows or the dots to move through them, and switch any capture between Light and Dark.

Use the previous and next buttons, the pagination dots, a horizontal swipe, or the left and right arrow keys to move between screens.

    Guided checks for parts a settings page cannot test

    A specifications page can tell you that a phone has a display, a speaker and sensors. It cannot tell you whether the touch panel responds correctly in every area, whether a sensor is working, or whether a speaker sounds distorted. Device Matrix adds guided tests for those practical checks.

    The tests cover the screen and touch input, sound, sensors, ports and radios. Each result can be seen as passed, failed or untested. You run the check yourself and decide what the result means. The test does not replace a repair technician, calibration equipment or a manufacturer’s diagnostic service.

    This is most useful when a problem is intermittent. A failed test gives you a clearer description to take to support: which component failed, what you were asked to do and what happened. A passed test does not prove that hardware is perfect, but it does give you a useful starting point.

    Understand apps, permissions and data use

    The Apps section looks at the apps installed on the phone, not just how much space they take. The analyzer can show information such as the Android API level an app targets, where it was installed from and which permissions it declares. Screen-time information adds context about daily use where Android makes it available.

    None of these details is a simple safety verdict. An older target API can be a reason to check for updates, but it does not by itself prove that an app is unsafe. A permission list shows what an app says it can request, not whether it has used that access inappropriately. Installer information can help identify an app that did not come from a store, but side-loaded apps are not automatically malicious.

    Treat the screens as a way to ask better questions: Is this app still maintained? Do I recognise the installer? Does a simple calculator really need access to my contacts? If something looks unusual, check the app’s source and permissions in Android settings before deciding what to do.

    Reports, exports and widgets

    A reading is more useful when you can keep or share it. Device Matrix can build a report from selected sections, show a preview, and then copy the text or share it through Android’s share sheet. The standard PDF is available on Free. Pro adds a custom PDF export and a sanitised JSON file.

    The preview matters. You can inspect what the report contains before it leaves the phone. Network names and addresses are masked by default, and the standard report is designed to avoid identifying details. Even so, review any export yourself before sending it to a support agent, a buyer or anyone else.

    Widgets put a smaller version of the same information on the home screen. Free includes 2×1 and 4×1 layouts for RAM, storage and battery. Pro adds 2×2 and 4×2 layouts, including the Device Health Check. Widgets are added from the launcher’s widget picker rather than from inside the app.

    What Device Matrix does not do

    The app is an informational utility, not an all-purpose repair kit. It does not remove malware, clean memory, extend battery life automatically, recover deleted files or guarantee that a phone is healthy. It does not monitor another person’s device or upload readings to a dashboard.

    • It depends on Android and the manufacturer. Some readings and tests are unavailable on some devices. “Not available on this device” is a real answer.
    • Some values are estimates. Battery health, device age and similar readings can be approximate and are marked accordingly.
    • A test is not a repair diagnosis. Passing a guided check is useful evidence, not a guarantee that a component will keep working.
    • It is not a security scanner. App API, installer and permission details are context for your own review, not a malware verdict.
    • Advertising and purchases use Google services. The app’s own readings stay local, but Google AdMob and Google Play operate under their own policies.

    How it can help the phone you use

    The value is not in seeing a long list of numbers. It is in using the right reading at the right moment. These are practical examples.

    1. Check a new or used phone

      Before buying or selling, review the model, Android version, storage, memory and battery details, then run the guided hardware checks. A clearer picture can expose a mismatch between the listing and the device.

    2. Troubleshoot a slow or hot phone

      Compare memory use, storage availability, thermal readings and the apps that are installed. This does not fix the problem, but it helps you decide whether storage, an app or the hardware deserves a closer look.

    3. Understand storage and battery behaviour

      See how much storage is free, how RAM is being used and what the phone reports about battery state. Use that context before deleting files or changing settings.

    4. Review network and data use

      Check the active connection, operator details and data-usage summaries. This can help when a phone is using more data than expected or when Wi-Fi behaves differently in one place.

    5. Look more closely at installed apps

      Use target API, installer and permission details to decide which apps deserve an update, a closer review or removal. Treat the information as a prompt for checking, not as an automatic judgement.

    6. Keep a record for support or resale

      Build a report, inspect the preview and share the result with a support agent or buyer. A consistent record is easier to discuss than a collection of screenshots.

    A simple resale check

    Open the dashboard and hardware screens, run the guided tests, check the network and sensor sections, then build a report preview. Share only the sections you are comfortable disclosing, and remember that a test result is evidence rather than a guarantee.

    Plans, privacy and what stays local

    The standard information screens, every guided test and the local reports are free. Remove ads is an optional one-time Google Play purchase that stops advertising. Pro adds the Wi-Fi analyzer, larger widgets, custom exports, dashboard layout and the Animations Off option. The website does not display prices; Google Play shows the local price and handles payment.

    Device Matrix does not ask for a login and does not run its own analytics service. Ads are provided through Google AdMob, and purchases are processed by Google Play. The app’s privacy policy explains those services, the permissions involved and the report-sharing choices in more detail.

    The app is informational, so its reports should be read with the same care as any other technical summary. Use the preview, mask what you do not need to share and keep sensitive details out of public messages.

    Try Device Matrix on Google Play

    Key takeaways

    • Device Matrix groups Android hardware, software, network and app readings into clear, local screens.
    • Guided checks and local reports help you investigate a problem or prepare a used phone for sale, but they are not repair or security verdicts.
    • App API, installer and permission details are useful context for a privacy review, not automatic proof that an app is safe or unsafe.
    • Readings stay on the phone. Reports leave only when you choose to copy, share, save or export them.
    • The core information screens and guided tests are free; Remove ads and Pro are optional one-time Google Play purchases.
